title: Tickets
description: One ticket per customer issue, with response targets and a full history.

A chat holds the whole conversation with a customer. A **ticket** is one issue inside it. A chat has at most one open ticket.

## When a ticket opens

- The AI hands the chat over.
- Someone on your team takes over or replies.
- The customer writes again after their last issue was resolved (see below).
- You create one yourself.

## Status

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Open** | Someone on your team needs to act. |
| **Waiting on customer** | You're waiting for the customer. The response-target clock pauses. |
| **Resolved** | Done. |

Priorities are **Urgent**, **High**, **Normal** and **Low**.

## When the customer writes again

- **Waiting on customer:** the ticket goes back to **Open** and the clock resumes where it stopped.
- **Resolved in the last 3 days:** the same ticket reopens. Its deadlines and escalation start again.
- **Resolved earlier than that:** a new ticket opens.
- **The AI is holding the chat:** nothing opens. The AI answers.

## Response targets and escalation

Targets for the first reply and for resolving are set in **Settings › Hours & response targets**. They count business hours only. Each ticket shows how long is left.

When a target is at risk, **escalation** alerts the people you chose, in the order you set, once per level. Set this up in **Settings › Escalation**.

## Working with many tickets

- Filter, search and sort the list. The filters are kept in the address, so you can share a view.
- Select several tickets to assign, change priority or resolve them together. If some couldn't be changed, the console tells you which ones and why.
- Open a ticket in the side panel to see its full history: who did what, and when.

When someone leaves the workspace, their open tickets are unassigned, and nothing new is routed to them.
