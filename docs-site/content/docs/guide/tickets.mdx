Tickets turn a chat into a tracked issue with an owner, a priority and deadlines, so nothing waits unanswered.

![The Tickets list with views, filters, due times and bulk actions](/screens/tickets.webp)

A chat holds the whole conversation with a customer. A **ticket** is one issue inside it. A chat has at most one open ticket.

## Before you start

- Everyone on the team can see and work on tickets. Moving a ticket that belongs to someone else needs a supervisor, admin or owner. Agents (team members) can assign tickets to themselves or unassign their own.
- Response targets and escalation are set by an admin under **Settings**. See [Settings](/docs/guide/settings).

## When a ticket opens

- The AI agent hands the chat over.
- Someone on your team takes over or replies in the [Inbox](/docs/guide/inbox).
- The customer writes again after their last issue was resolved (see below).
- You create one yourself.

To create one, open a chat, then click **Create ticket** in the details panel. Give it a **Subject** (leave it empty to use the customer's last message) and a **Priority**. Routing rules pick who gets it, unless the chat already has someone.

## Status and priority

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Open** | Someone on your team needs to act. |
| **Waiting on customer** | You're waiting for the customer. The response-target clock pauses. |
| **Resolved** | Done. |

Priorities are **Urgent**, **High**, **Normal** and **Low**.

## When the customer writes again

- **Waiting on customer:** the ticket goes back to **Open** and the clock resumes where it stopped.
- **Resolved in the last 3 days:** the same ticket reopens. Its deadlines and escalation start again from the beginning.
- **Resolved earlier than that:** a new ticket opens.
- **The agent holds the chat:** no ticket opens, and the agent answers.

Handing a chat back to the agent resolves its ticket, without a rating question.

## Work the list

Pick a view across the top: **Open**, **Overdue**, **Escalated**, **Waiting on customer**, **Resolved** or **All**. Then narrow it:

- **Search** by number, subject or customer with the omnibar in the top bar.
- **Assignee:** **Anyone**, **Assigned to me**, **Unassigned**, or a person.
- **Priority** and **Team**. A link can also filter by a tag, shown as **Tag: …** above the list.
- **Sort:** **Due soonest** (the default for work still to do) or **Newest** (the default for **Resolved** and **All**).

Your filters are kept in the page address, so you can copy the link and a colleague sees the same list. Links from [Analytics](/docs/guide/analytics) and emails open filtered the same way. The list refreshes by itself and loads 100 tickets at a time.

### The Due column

| You see | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **First reply due in 25m** | No reply has gone out yet; this is the time left to the first-reply target. |
| **Resolve due in 3h** | The first reply is done; this is the time left to the resolution target. |
| **First reply overdue by 10m** (red) | The target has passed. |
| **Paused: waiting on customer** | The clock is stopped until the customer writes. |
| **Met** | Resolved on time. |
| **Resolved late** | Resolved after the target. |

The time turns darker when less than 30 minutes are left. Targets count business hours only, set in **Settings › Hours & targets**.

### Bulk actions

Tick the tickets you want (or the box in the header to select all on screen), and a bar appears: **Assign to…**, **Set priority…**, **Move to team…** and **Resolve**. The console tells you what happened, for example "3 tickets reassigned; 1 couldn't be." and lists the reasons. The tickets that failed stay selected so you can fix and retry them.

## Open a ticket

![A ticket's side panel with status, assignee, tags, escalations and history](/screens/ticket-sheet.webp)

Click a row to open the side panel. From there:

- Edit the **Subject**, **Status** (**Open** or **Waiting on customer**), **Priority**, assignee, team and **Tags**.
- See how it started, from "The AI agent handed the chat over" to "Created by hand".
- Read **Escalations** (which levels fired) and **History**: who did what, and when, including the before and after of each change.
- Click **Open chat** to jump to the conversation.
- Click **Resolve**, or **Reopen** on a resolved ticket.

## Resolve a ticket

**Resolve** opens a short dialog:

| Option | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **What fixed it (optional)** | A note kept on the ticket and shown under **How it was resolved**. |
| **Ask the customer how we did** | Sends **Good**, **Okay** and **Bad** buttons on WhatsApp, if your workspace asks for ratings. Not possible once the 24-hour reply window is closed. |
| **Hand the chat back to the AI agent** | The agent answers the customer's next message. Turn it off to keep the chat with your team. |

When several tickets are resolved together, ratings go only to customers who wrote in the last 24 hours.

If your workspace asks for ratings, the customer's answer is saved on the ticket and shown beside it in the chat's ticket list. Customers can answer for up to 3 days after you ask.

## Response targets and escalation

Targets for the first reply and for resolving are set in **Settings › Hours & targets**. When a target is at risk, **escalation** alerts the people you chose, in the order you set, once per level, by bell and optionally email. Set it up in **Settings › Escalation**. Reopening a ticket restarts its deadlines, and escalation starts again from the first level.

<Callout type="info">
When someone leaves the workspace, their open tickets are unassigned, and nothing new is routed to them.
</Callout>

## If something goes wrong

| What you see | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| "No tickets match these filters." | Click **Clear filters**. |
| Part of a bulk change failed | Read the reasons in the message, fix them (often a permission), and run it again on the tickets still selected. |
| **Ask the customer how we did** is greyed out | The 24-hour reply window is closed, so a rating question can't be sent. |

## Frequently asked questions

<Accordions type="single">
<Accordion title="Why did a ticket open when nobody created one?">
A ticket opens on its own when the AI agent hands a chat over, when someone on your team takes over or replies, and when a customer writes again after their issue was resolved. See [When a ticket opens](#when-a-ticket-opens).
</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Why did a resolved ticket come back?">
The customer wrote again within 3 days of it being resolved, so the same ticket reopened with fresh deadlines. After 3 days, a new ticket opens instead.
</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Do deadlines keep running while we wait for the customer?">
No. Setting a ticket to **Waiting on customer** pauses its deadlines and escalation, and they carry on from where they stopped when the customer writes.
</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Do deadlines count nights and weekends?">
Only if your workspace is open then. Targets count business hours, set in [Settings › Hours & targets](/docs/guide/settings#hours--targets).
</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Can a chat have two open tickets?">
No. A chat has at most one open ticket. If you try to create another, the console points you to the one that's already open.
</Accordion>
</Accordions>
